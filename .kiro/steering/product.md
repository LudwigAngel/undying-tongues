# Product: Undying Tongues (Lenguas que no mueren)

## Problem
A language dies when its last fluent generation goes quiet. In Peru, Quechua has millions of
speakers but children in cities stop using it. Elders hold the pronunciation, the words, the jokes;
apps like Duolingo do not cover most Indigenous languages and never use the family's own voice.

## Users
1. **Elders** (60+, low digital literacy, often more comfortable speaking than reading). They record.
2. **Children and grandchildren** (8-16) who understand a little and are losing it. They practise.
3. **Community teachers** who want material in their local variety, not a textbook standard.

## Core loop
Elder records a word in their own voice -> the word becomes a candle on the altar -> the child
practises; each correct recall keeps the flame burning -> the flame fades as the child forgets
(forgetting curve) -> the altar shows the vitality of the language in this family.

## Hackathon framing (Kiroween 2026, category: Resurrection)
The Halloween theme carries meaning, it is not decoration: Día de los Muertos / Todos los Santos
altars, voices of the ancestors, candles that go out when a word is forgotten. Keep it respectful:
no zombies, skulls or mockery around Indigenous people or their dead.

## Principles
- Voice first. Every screen must be usable by someone who reads slowly.
- The family's variety is the reference. Never "correct" an elder's recording to a standard.
- Offline and on-device by default. Rural connectivity is poor and voices are personal data.
- Bilingual UI (English / Spanish) and the target language never translated away.
